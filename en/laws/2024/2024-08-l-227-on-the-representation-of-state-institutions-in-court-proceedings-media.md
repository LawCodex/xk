### CHAPTER I

### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

Purpose This law defines the rules of representation of state administration institutions in court proceedings, mediation and arbitration. It, also, regulates the functioning and competencies of the State Advocacy Office, as well as the legal status of the municipal advocate.

#### Article 2

Scope

\1. The provisions of this Law:

1.1. are implemented for the representation of state administration institutions, including the Office of the Prime Minister, ministries, executive agencies, public service administration bodies;

1.2. are implemented by the State Advocacy Office and Municipal Advocates, only if otherwise provided by special laws;

1.3. are not implemented in cases when parties to court proceedings are independent institutions, regulatory agencies, as well as cases when both parties to the proceedings are state administration bodies.

\2. In cases referred to in sub-paragraph 1.3. of this Article, the independent institutions by request may ask to be represented by State Advocacy Office.

#### Article 3

Definitions

\1. For purposes of this Law, the terms used shall have the following meanings:

1.1. State Advocate – an employee of the State Advocacy Office, in the capacity of an attorney, who represents the interests of state institutions in court proceedings, mediation and arbitration.

1.2. Municipality Advocate – an employee in the local self-governance unit, in the capacity of

1.3. Advice - giving a written opinion by the State Advocacy Office to state institutions, when required by them, in particular to initiate, challenge or withdraw cases from court proceedings, mediation and arbitration.

1.4. Representation - performance of all legal actions on behalf and on the account of state institutions in court proceedings, mediation and arbitration.

#### Article 4

Operational principles

\1. State Advocacy Office, during the representation of state administration institutions in court proceedings, mediation and arbitration, shall act in accordance with the following principles:

1.1.legality and transparency;

1.2.economy and efficiency;

1.3.responsibility and accountability;

1.4.professional independence and non-discrimination.

### CHAPTER II

### LEGAL STATUS, COMPETENCIES AND ORGANIZATION OF THE STATE ADVOCACY

### OFFICE

#### Article 5

Legal Status, Headquarters, Stamp and Emblem

\1. The State Advocacy Office has the status of a legal entity; it is an Executive Agency within the Ministry responsible for Justice.

\2. The State Advocacy Office, has its emblem which is proposed by the Executive Director and approved by the Minister.

\3. The State Advocacy Office has its headquarters in Prishtina.

\4. The State Advocacy Office has its stamp, its form and diameter shall be defined according to the relevant provisions on stamps in the institutions of the Republic of Kosovo.

#### Article 6

Organization of the State Advocacy Office

\1. State Advocacy Office shall have no less than ten (10) state advocates.

\2. State advocates are graduated lawyers with over ten (10) years of work experience as lawyers.

\3. State Advocates have passed the bar exam, are persons of high moral and professional integrity and work based on the principles of transparency, accountability and efficiency.

\4. State Advocacy Office has a supporting staff who have university degrees, at least five (5) years of work experience in legal matters, have university degree in law, have professional and moral integrity and master the English language at the level of professional use.

\5. The organizational structure of the State Advocacy Office, shall be regulated by regulation on the internal organization of the State Advocacy Office.

#### Article 7

Public work

\1. The work of State Advocacy Office is public, with the exception of the mediation and proceedings before the international arbitration which, by nature, are confidential.

\2. State Advocacy Office acts in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Kosovo and, with the exception of classified processes, sensitive processes for the interests of the Republic of Kosova, as well as those referred to in paragraph 1. of this Article, provide access to public documents.

#### Article 8

Language The official languages provided by the Constitution and law, including the professional level of English, are the official languages in use in the State Advocacy Office work.

#### Article 9

Competencies of the State Advocacy Office

\1. The State Advocacy Office shall have the following competencies:

1.1 represents and defends the state administration institutions, as defined by the relevant legislation on Public Financial Management and Accountability, in court proceeding, mediation and arbitration in accordance with the Law;

1.2. represents and defends the Republic of Kosovo or its institutions in court proceedings in international courts, mediation and international arbitration;

1.3. cooperates with the State Prosecutor in defending state interests for civil cases which are resolved through a criminal process;

1.4. provides written advices from its scope to the bodies of state institutions.

1.5. acts based on the principles of legality, efficiency, transparency and accountability and is responsible for every delay which causes damage to the institutions of the Republic of Kosova and the state of Kosova;

1.6. assesses the risk of draft contracts sent by public authorities and gives an opinion related to their legality;

1.7. performs other tasks provided by law.

\2. The category of draft contracts, as well as the content of the opinion according to sub-paragraph

1.6., of paragraph 1. of this Article, is determined by a sub-legal act which is approved by the Government upon the proposal of the Ministry of Justice.

\2. Hiring services from paragraph 1. of this Article shall be carried out in accordance with the relevant provisions of the Law on Public Procurement, except when the confidentiality of the procedure imposes another action.

#### Article 12

State Advocate General

\1. The State Advocate General shall lead and represent the State Advocacy Office, and practice their function in the State Advocacy Office by leading and undertaking actions for which they are authorized by law.

\2. Advocate General is a lawyer, who has passed the bar exam, with more than ten (10) years of legal work experience, high moral and professional integrity, independent from influence or political engagement, proficient in English language, and has high communication skills.

\3. The State Advocate General assigns duties to State Advocates and gives them general instructions related to performance of duties, in accordance with the State Advocacy Office Regulation on internal organization and systematization of job positions.

\4. The State Advocate General may assign State Advocates with additional work and duties, which may be of an urgent nature in the public interest.

\5. The State Advocate General is independent in performing his/her function.

\6. The State Advocate General may, after the approval of the Minister of Justice, authorize the person employed in the institutions from sub-paragraph 1.1. of Article 2 of this Law, who has passed the bar exam and works in legal affairs, to represent the relevant institution in courts and other bodies in legal property disputes.

\7. For purposes of this Article, instructions for the work of state advocates shall include general guidelines on the performance of duties and activities, as well as guidelines for undertaking actions and procedures in specific cases.

#### Article 13

Deputy State Advocate General

\1. The Deputy Advocate General assists the State Advocate General in leading the State Advocacy Office, as well as performs the duties and responsibilities of the Advocate General when he or she is absent.

\2. The Deputy Advocate General has full authority to act on behalf of the State Advocacy Office in case of absence or incapacity of the Advocate General. Deputy Advocate General has same qualifications and responsibilities as the State Advocate.

\3. The Deputy Advocate General also performs other tasks which are delegated to him/her by the State Advocate General.

#### Article 14

State Advocates

\1. State Advocates of the State Advocacy Office exercise the function of state advocates.

\2. In performing representation jobs, State Advocates are independent and authorized to take all actions which a party to the proceeding undertakes, for purposes of protecting the interests of the party they represent, in accordance with the applicable legislation.

\3. State Advocates inform the State Advocate General regularly on the situation and developments of the cases they have been assigned.

\4. The State Advocate reports regularly to the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 15

Reporting of the State Advocate General

\1. The State Advocate General submits a weekly report to the Minister of Justice regarding the situation and outcome of the case representation.

\2. In addition to the reports according to paragraph 1. of this Article, the State Advocate General reports in other periods when requested by the Minister.

\3. The State Advocate General must inform the Minister about all ongoing proceedings, in particular those related to arbitration proceedings, as well as those of state interest.

\4. The report should contain all the information regarding the real situation of case representation, activities and works performed within the field of activity by state advocates.

#### Article 16

Disqualification Rules of contested procedure for disqualification of judges apply accordingly also for the disqualification of the State Advocate General, the Municipal Advocate, the Deputy State Advocate General and the State Advocates.

#### Article 17

Conflict of interest In exercising his/her public functions, the State Advocate General, the Municipality Advocate, the Deputy State Advocate General as well as any State Advocate may not represent the public authorities if he has a personal interest, direct or indirect, in a case.

#### Article 18

Incompatibility

\1. The Advocate General, the Deputy State Advocate General and the state advocates may not be members of the bodies of any political party nor exercise political, official or private professional activities that conflict with the function they are exercising.

\2. The Advocate General, the Deputy State Advocate General as well as the state advocates may engage in scientific, cultural, academic and other activities, which do not conflict with their functions and the legislation in force.

#### Article 19

Representation costs Representation costs by the State Advocate General and State Advocates shall be covered by the State Advocacy Office, and by the municipality for representation of the local level.

#### Article 20

Obligation to cooperate

\1. In cases of representing state administration bodies or independent institutions, according to the provisions of this law, the relevant body is obliged to cooperate and respond to the requests of the State Advocacy Office, in accordance with the legal or judicial deadlines.

\2. All officials, civil servants, employees and contractors of the relevant authorities who are explicitly obliged to fully cooperate with the State Advocacy Office must respond to its requests for documents or information.

\3. In case any state administration institutions does not respond to the request of the State Advocacy Office, the Advocacy through the Minister of Justice shall notify the Prime Minister and the head of the relevant institution, who has not cooperated with the Advocacy.

### CHAPTER III

### APPOINTMENT AND DISMISSAL FROM FUNCTION

#### Article 21

Appointment of the State Advocate General and State Advocates

\1. The State Advocate General and the Deputy State Advocate General are not public officials and shall be appointed and dismissed by the Minister of Justice.

\2. The mandate of the Advocate General and deputy the Deputy State Advocate is seven (7) years.

\3. The Advocate General and deputy Advocate General shall be dismissed by the Minister, only in cases of professional incapacity, misuse, mismanagement or in cases of damage caused directly to the state interests.

#### Article 22

Criteria for appointment

\1. Any citizen who meets the following criteria may be appointed as a State Advocate:

1.1. has a university degree in law;

### CHAPTER IV

### EMPLOYEES OF THE STATE ADVOCACY OFFICE

#### Article 23

The personnel in the State Advocacy Office

\1. In the State Advocacy Office, there are a certain number of public officials who perform professional and technical-administrative duties.

\2. The State Advocacy Office may employ a certain number of professional collaborators who help the office-holders to perform legal work. Such professional collaborators must have completed the master level of studies, with at least three (3) years of work experience in legal matters, and should be able to speak and use English language.

\3. The number of public officials at the State Advocacy Office shall be determined by the act on internal organization and systematization of job positions of the State Advocacy Office.

\4. Employees at the State Advocacy Office are public officials and for personnel issues the provisions of the relevant Law on Civil Service shall apply accordingly.

#### Article 24

Professional training

\1. The State Advocate General, Deputy State Advocate General, state advocates and civil servants in the State Advocacy Office shall have the right and duty to continuous professional training and education.

\2. For professional training and education needs, necessary means are provided which are foreseen in the Budget of the Ministry of Justice and allocated for the State Advocacy Office.

#### Article 25

Evaluation of professional work

\1. Every six (6) months, State Advocates shall be subject to a professional work evaluation procedure which is conducted by the State Advocate General, which shall take into consideration results achieved in litigations and representations performed during the period of evaluation. The evaluation is supervised and approved by the Minister of Justice.

\2. The assessment ascertaining professional incompetence constitutes grounds for initiating the dismissal of a state advocate.

### CHAPTER V

### REPRESENTATION OF LOCAL LEVEL

#### Article 26

Municipal Advocate

\1. The municipal advocate shall represent and defend the municipality in all cases where the municipality appears as a party in the procedure.

\2. The Municipal Advocate shall be appointed by an open, public competition by the Mayor, in accordance with the provisions of the Law on Public Officials.

\3. The Municipal Advocate shall act independently in the functional and administrative sense, in accordance with the Law on Local Self-Governance and for their work reports to the Mayor.

#### Article 27

Implementation Accordingly The provisions of this law applicable to state advocates, where possible, shall apply accordingly to municipal advocates as well.
