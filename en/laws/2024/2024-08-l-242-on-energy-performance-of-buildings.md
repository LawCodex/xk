# LAW NO. 08/L-242 ON ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS

### LAW NO. 08/L-242 ON ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS

### LAW NO. 08/L-242

### ON ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS

The Assembly of the Republic of Kosovo;

Based on Article 65 (1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Kosovo, Approves:

### LAW ON ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS

#### Article 1

Purpose

\1. The purpose of this Law is to determine rules, procedures and measures in order to improve the energy performance of buildings, taking into account outdoor climatic and local conditions, as well as indoor climate requirements and cost-effectiveness.

\2. This Law is partially aligned with the Directive 2010/31/EU on the Energy Performance of Buildings and Directive (EU) 2018/844 amending Directive 2010/31/EU on the energy performance of buildings.

#### Article 2

Scope of Application

\1. The provisions of this Law apply to all existing buildings and new buildings that are included in the process of energy performance of buildings in the territory of the Republic of Kosovo.

\2. This Law shall not apply to:

2.1. buildings officially protected as part of a designated environment or because of their special architectural or historical merit, in so far as compliance with certain minimum energy performance requirements would unacceptably alter their character or appearance;

2.2. buildings used as places of worship and for religious activities;

2.3. temporary buildings with a time of use for two (2) years or less, industrial sites, workshops and non-residential agricultural buildings with low energy demand and non-residential agricultural buildings, which are in use by any sector covered by a national sectorial agreement on energy performance;

2.4. residential buildings which are used or intended to be used for either less than four (4)

months of the year or, for a limited annual time of use and with an expected energy consumption of less than twenty-five (25) % of what would be the result of all-year use;

2.5. stand-alone buildings with a total useful floor area of less than fifty (50) m 2.

1.1.1. Existing building – building constructed before the start of implementation of this law;

1.1.2. New building – building constructed after the start of implementation of this law.

#### Article 4

Energy performance of buildings

\1. Energy performance of a building is the calculated amount of the energy which is expressed in a transparent and objective manner, including:

1.1. an energy performance indicator, energy class that is based on the conversion factors of final energy consumption into primary energy and which is premised on the national annual weighted averages or on a specific value for on-site production; and

1.2. a numerical indicator for primary energy use.

#### Article 5

National Calculation Methodology

\1. National Calculation Methodology, (hereinafter NCM), presents the methodology for calculating the integrated energy performance of a building. NCM is used in the calculation of cost-optimal levels of energy performance requirements for buildings, building units and building elements, which is used for the preparation and issue of Energy Performance Certificates (hereinafter EPCs);

\2. The Ministry with sub-legal act shall define the NCM, which:

2.1. is used to calculate the annual energy required to meet the different needs of a building associated with its typical use;

2.2. produces an energy performance indicator as a numeric indicator of primary energy;

2.3. is used to calculate cost-optimal levels of energy performance demands for buildings, building units and building elements;

2.4. is used to prepare and issue the EPC;

2.5. takes into account all relevant European Standards.

\3. NCM will cover, as a minimum, the following classes of buildings when evaluating energy performance for:

3.1. single-family houses of different types;

3.2. multi-apartment blocks;

#### Article 6

Determination of Minimum energy performance requirements

\1. Minimum energy performance requirements will apply to:

1.1. construction of new buildings;

1.2. existing buildings and building units undergoing major renovations;

1.3. building elements that form part of the building envelope and which have a significant impact on the energy performance of the building envelope when they are replaced or retrofitted;

1.4. technical building systems, whenever they are installed, replaced or advanced.

\2. The cost-optimal level will be considered during the determination of minimum energy performance requirements of buildings which leads to the lowest cost during the estimated economic lifecycle;

\3. Minimum energy performance requirements for buildings or building units shall be reviewed at regular intervals which shall not be longer than five (5) years and, if necessary, shall be updated in order to reflect technical progress in the building sector.

\4. Within eighteen (18) months after the entry into force of this Law:

4.1. all new buildings will be required to comply with the appropriate minimum energy performance requirements;

4.2. all buildings undergoing major renovation will be required to comply with the appropriate minimum energy performance requirements.

\5. The Ministry, by sub-legal act, determines the minimum energy performance requirements for buildings, based on NCM.

#### Article 8

The Nearly Zero-Energy Buildings

\1. After approval of NBRS, the Ministry, in cooperation with the Ministry in charge of energy, will draw up the national plan for increasing the number of nearly zero-energy buildings, (hereinafter: NZEB National Plan), and shall propose it to the Government for approval.

\2. The target objective for the new buildings to meet the requirement for near-zero energy consumption will be no later than 31 December 2030.

\3. The target objective for the new buildings in use and owned by the public authorities to meet the requirement for nearly-zero energy consumption will be no later than 31 December 2028.

\4. NZEB National Plan will set out:

4.1. the detailed definition and technical features of nearly zero-energy buildings, with a reference to a numerical indicator of primary energy use, expressed in kWh/m2 per year;

4.2. intermediate targets for improving the energy performance of new buildings;

4.3. different targets depending on the category of a building;

4.4. information on additional policies and measures needed to achieve these targets, including details of national requirements and measures concerning the use of energy from renewable sources in new buildings and existing buildings undergoing major renovation.

\5. NZEB National Plan may be updated every three (3) years and be submitted to the Secretariat of the Energy Community in accordance with the Energy Community’s reporting requirements.

#### Article 10

Use of high-efficiency alternative systems

\1. When designing a new building or when an existing building or building unit undergoes a major renovation, the possible use of at least the following high-performance systems must be evaluated:

1.1. decentralized energy supply systems using renewable energy sources;

1.2. systems using co-generation;

1.3. systems using heat pumps;

1.4. central heating and cooling systems, especially those using renewable energy sources supplied with power from a central energy source for a number of buildings or sites.

\2. When evaluating the use of any of the high-efficiency alternative systems, the following factors must be estimated and be considered:

2.1. technical, environmental and economic considerations;

2.2. issues of healthy and indoor climate conditions;

2.3. fire safety, and

2.4. risks related to intense seismic activity.

\3. Evidence of the evaluation of these alternative measures must be included with the construction documents submitted with the application for a construction permit.

1.2. for buildings or building units undergoing major renovation;

#### Article 16

Independent control system for EPCs

\1. An independent control system for issued EPCs shall be managed by the Ministry.

\2. The purpose of this system is to verify the EPCs by a combination of the following:

2.1. a check on the validity of the input data of the building used to issue the EPC, plus confirmation that the results stated in the EPC and the EPC report are in the correct format;

2.2. additional verification of the accuracy of the calculated results and of the recommendations made;

2.3. an on-site visit of the building to verify the accuracy of the information set forth in the EPC and the accompanying EPC report of the building certified.

\3. The Ministry will make a random selection of at least a statistically significant percentage of all the EPCs, and EPC reports issued in a given year and verified independently in accordance with the requirements set forth in the sub legal act.

\4. The EPCs and the accompanying EPC reports need to be made available by the authorized energy assessor, who has prepared the EPC that is subject to independent control, to the Ministry on request.

\5. The Ministry, by means of a sub-legal act, shall determine the selection process of the controllers.

During the time that the person performs the task of the controller, (s)he may not perform the tasks of the energy assessor.

\1. Heating and air conditioning systems inspection – a mandatory inspection at regular intervals by an independent expert to determine the condition of the system and to prepare inspection report.

2.1. heating systems with boilers and systems for combined space heating and ventilation with an effective rated output for space heating greater than seventy (70) kW; and

2.2. air-conditioning systems with an effective rated output of more than seventy (70) kW.

4.1. no changes have been made to the heating or the air-conditioning system or to the system for combined space heating/air-conditioning and ventilation or to the heating or cooling requirements of the building following an inspection that has already been carried out;

4.2. technical building systems are explicitly covered by an agreed energy performance criterion or a contractual arrangement specifying an agreed level of energy efficiency improvement, such as energy performance contracting, or are operated by a utility or network operator and therefore are subject to performance monitoring measures on the system side;

4.3. non-residential buildings are equipped with building automation and control systems;

4.4. residential buildings are equipped with:

4.4.1. effective functionality of continuous electronic monitoring that measures systems’ efficiency and informs building owners or managers when it has fallen significantly and when system servicing is necessary; and

4.4.2. effective control functionality to ensure optimum generation, distribution, storage and use of energy.

\5. Where technically and economically feasible, non-residential buildings with an effective rated output for heating systems or systems for combined space heating and ventilation of over two hundred ninety (290) kW, or with an effective rated output for air-conditioning systems for combined air-conditioning and ventilation systems of over two hundred ninety (290) kW, will be equipped with building automation and control systems, which shall be capable of:

5.1. continuously monitoring, logging, analysing and allowing for adjusting energy use;

5.2. benchmarking the building’s energy efficiency, detecting decreases in efficiency of technical building systems, and informing the person responsible for the facilities or technical building management about opportunities for energy efficiency improvement; and

5.3. communication with connected technical building systems and other appliances inside the building, and being interoperable with technical building systems across different types of proprietary technologies, devices and manufacturers.

\6. For all inspections, a report shall be drawn up by an independent expert and be submitted to the Ministry, which shall:

6.1. include an assessment of the system efficiency and sizing compared with the heating or cooling requirements of the building;

6.2. contain recommendations to improve the energy performance of the system, if they are cost-effective in terms of the planned lifetime of the building;

6.3. contain a comparison of the energy performance of the system compared with the best available feasible system, and with an equivalent system complying with all relevant current regulations;

6.4. be handed also to the owner or tenant of the building and to the relevant institution referenced in any EPC, if the building undergoes energy certification.

\7. The Ministry, by means of a sub-legal act, shall determine the process for the inspection of heating and air conditioning systems, taxes, procedures, content and follow-up report of the inspection.

\8. As an alternative to the inspection of heating and air-conditioning systems referred to in paragraph

\1. of this Article, the Ministry in coordination with the Ministry in charge of energy - may design and implement alternative measures to ensure the provision of advice to users concerning the replacement of heat generators or air-conditioning systems or systems for combined air-conditioning and ventilation, other modifications any such system, and alternative solutions to assess the efficiency and appropriate size of any of those systems.

\9. Every three (3) years a report shall be submitted to the Energy Community Secretariat, based on request, which among others:

9.1. shows that the overall energy saving impact of the alternative measures is equivalent or greater than inspections;

9.2. includes quantified evidence or projections as supporting evidence.

#### Article 18

Independent control system for heating and air-conditioning system inspection reports

\1. An independent control system for heating and air-conditioning system inspection reports shall be managed by the Ministry.

\2. The purpose of this system is:

2.1. to confirm that the heating and air-conditioning system inspection reports are recorded in the correct format;

2.2. to assess the validity of the input data of the inspection reports; and

2.3. to verify the accuracy of the results of the inspection reports and the recommendations made.

\3. The Ministry will make a random selection of at least a statistically significant percentage of all heating and air-conditioning system inspection reports submitted annually and verified in accordance with the requirements set forth in the sub legal act.

\4. The heating and air-conditioning system inspection reports as well as all data and records relating to any such report need to be made available by the independent expert or inspection company, who has prepared the said report that is subject to independent control, to the Ministry on request.

\5. The Ministry selects the controllers, through a competitive and transparent process according to the sub-legal act foreseen in Article 14, paragraph 3. and Article 17 paragraph 7. of this Law. During the time that the person performs the task of the controller, he may not perform the tasks of the energy assessor and the independent expert.

#### Article 19

The building owner’s obligations

\1. The owner of the building is obliged:

1.1. to perform for the whole building, or for building units as part of it, building energy certification;

1.2. to commission regular heating and/or air conditioning systems inspection where applicable according to this law;

1.3. to provide access to the building and its facilities for carrying-out the on-site inspections by the competent authorities;

1.4. to make available to the competent authorities, on request, all requisite data and information, including inspection reports on the building’s heating and air-conditioning systems;

1.5. to ensure that the building after any major renovation is compliant with the minimum energy performance requirements with respect to the building, building unit and elements;

1.8. to ensure that new, replaced or upgraded technical building systems in existing buildings that comply with the system requirements foreseen under Articles 10 and 11 of this Law.

#### Article 20

Minor offense Sanctions

\1. The following minor offense sanctions shall apply to any person who is bound by, and is in breach of, the provisions of Article 13 or Article 17 of this law:

1.1. any natural person and the responsible person of the legal entity shall be punished by fine of one thousand (1000) Euros up to two thousand (2000) Euros;

1.2. any natural person, who exercises an individual business, shall be punished by fine of three thousand (3000) Euros up to five thousand (5000) Euros;

1.3. any legal entity shall be punished by fine from ten thousand (10,000) Euros to twenty thousand (20,000) Euros.

\2. The amount of the fine shall depend on:

2.1. the surface area and use of the relevant building or building unit;

2.2. the size of the building’s installed energy systems, and/or

2.3. the continuous recurrence of any breach.

\3. An energy assessor or an independent expert will be in breach of his/her obligations in any case where:

3.1. he/she allows unauthorised persons to use his/her authorisation;

3.2. he/she deliberately records inaccurate energy or other data on the EPC or on any heating or air-conditioning system inspection report,

3.3. an energy assessor requests the issue of an EPC and submits an EPC report, despite the fact that he/she has not actually carried out an EPC assessment;

3.4. an independent expert or an inspection company hands over a heating or air-conditioning system, despite the fact that no inspection has been actually carried out;

3.5. he/she submits inaccurate data and supporting documents;

3.6. he/she violates the duty of confidentiality and secrecy with regard to the use of the data and information collected during the performance of his/her work;

3.7. he/she fails to fulfil his/her professional duties and contractual obligations;

3.8. in the case of an inspection company:

3.8.1. any such company uses uncertified independent experts to conduct inspections;

3.8.2. the number of the independent certified experts being employed by such a company is lower than the number stipulated in the applicable legislation; and/or

\4. If, after an inspection conducted by the Ministry, it is established that an energy assessor or an independent expert is in breach of any of the obligations referred to in paragraph 3 of this Article, the offender shall:

#### Article 21

The right to Appeal

\1. Against any final decision according to this law an appeal is allowed in compliance with the rules defined in the relevant Law on General Administrative Procedure.

\2. The superior body that examines the complaint according to this Article is the Complaint Commission. The complaint commission is established by decision of the Minister.

3.The Minister, by sub-legal act, shall determine the composition of the commission and the mandate of the commission members. The work and procedure of the complaint commission is based on the rules determined for collegial bodies in the relevant Law on General Administrative Procedure.

#### Article 22

Issuance of sub-legal acts With a view to implementing this Law, the competent institutions shall issue sub-legal acts within eighteen (18) months, after entry into force of this Law.

#### Article 23

Repealing provisions

\1. Entry into force of this Law derogates Law No.05/L–101 on Energy Performance of Buildings.

\2. The current sub-legal acts are applied until new sub-legal acts are issued according to this Law.

#### Article 24

Entry into force This Law shall enter into force fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Kosovo.
