# LAW NO. 08/L-173 ON CYBER SECURITY

### LAW NO. 08/L-173

### ON CYBER SECURITY

Assembly of the Republic of Kosovo;

Based on Article 65 (1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Kosovo, Adopts:

### LAW ON CYBER SECURITY

### CHAPTER I

### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

Purpose

\1. This Law establishes the principles of cyber security, the institutions that develop, implement and promote cyber security policy, the responsibilities of the authorities in the field of cyber security, the duties of cyber security entities, inter-institutional cooperation, the prevention and combating of cybercrime in the Republic of Kosovo against any threat or attack and establishes the Cyber Security Agency.

\2. This Law partially transposes Directive (EU) 2013/40 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 August 2013 on attacks against information systems replacing Council Framework Decision 2005/222 / JHA as well as Directive (EU) 2016/1148 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 6 July 2016 on security measures for network and information systems.

#### Article 2

Scope This Law shall be implemented by the Cyber Security Agency and other institutional mechanisms of the Republic of Kosovo for the proposal, implementation of cyber measures as well as the guaranteeing the cyber security.

1.1. Cybernetics - means activities related to involvement of computers or computer networks, such as internet;

1.2. Cyberspace - means the complex environment that results from interaction of people, software and services on the internet through technological devices and networks connected to it, which does not exist in any physical form;

1.3. Cyber security - means necessary activities to protect network and information systems, users of such systems and other persons affected by a cyber incident;

1.4. Computer system - means a device or group of interconnected or related devices, one or more of which, pursuant to a program, perform automatic processing of digital data;

#### Article 4

Principles of Guaranteeing Cyber Security

\1. The following principles shall be taken into account in ensuring cyber security:

1.1. the principle of personality – which means that ensuring the security of a system will be arranged by the service provider;

### LAW NO. 08/L-173 ON CYBER SECURITY

1.2. the principle of integral protection - which means that the service provider will ascertain the potential risks posed to the system and apply appropriate organizational and technical measures for protection of the network and information system;

1.3. the principle of minimizing the adverse effect - which means that in the case of a cyber incident, the service provider shall implement due care and measures to avoid the escalation of the effect of the cyber incident and its possible spread to another system, and notify the supervisory authority provided under this cyber incident law;

1.4. the principle of cooperation - which means that in ensuring cyber security and resolving cyber incidents, the parties will cooperate and, if necessary, take into mutual connection between and dependence of the systems and services.

### CHAPTER II

### OBLIGATIONS FOR ENSURING CYBER SECURITY

#### Article 5

Security measures of the operator of essential services’ system

\1. The operator of essential services shall permanently apply organizational, physical and information technology security measures for:

1.1. preventing cyber incidents;

1.2. resolving cyber incidents;

1.3. preventing and mitigating an impact on the continuity of the service or the security of the system due to a cyber incident or for preventing and mitigating a possible impact on the continuity of another dependent service or the security of a system.

\2. Upon the implementation of security measures, the operator of essential services is required to:

2.1. prepare a system risk assessment that should:

2.1.1. set out a list of risks affecting security of the system and continuity of the service and causing the occurrence of cyber incidents;

2.1.2. determine the severity of consequences of a cyber incident occurring upon the realization of risks, based on the following parameters:

2.1.2.1. the number of users affected by the interruption of essential services;

2.1.2.2. duration of the cyber incident;

2.1.2.3. geographical extent in relation to the area affected by the cyber incident.

2.2. describe the measures for resolving a cyber incident;

2.3. ensure the existence and timeliness of a documented system risk assessment, security regulations and description of the application of security measures;

2.4. ensure the monitoring of the system for detecting actions or software compromising its security and communicate information about the actions or software compromising the security of the system to the CSA;

2.5. take measures for reducing the impact and spread of a cyber incident, including restriction of the use of or access to the system, if necessary;

2.6. check the sufficiency, effectiveness and compliance of the application of security measures and document the results;

2.7. retain the documents envisaged in sub-paragraph 2.6 of this Article for at least three

(3) years from their creation.

\3. If the operator of essential services authorizes another party to administer the system or uses another party to host the system, the operator of essential services shall be responsible for the application of the security measures of the system by the other party.

\4. The essential service operator is obliged to establish the CSIRT of OES or at least to appoint an official who will be in charge for network and information systems and security.

\5. The operator of essential services may be part of the national sectoral CSIRT, but this does not release it from the obligation set out in the paragraph 4 of this Article.

\6. The description of the security measures for the network and information system, used for the provision of an essential service and requirements for the preparation of the risk assessment, shall be determined by a sub-legal act approved by the Minister of the Ministry responsible for internal affairs.

\7. Failure to implement paragraphs 1 to 4 of this Article by the operators of essential services shall be determined as minor offence.

\8. The procedures and response measures in case of a cyber incident, are determined by a sub-legal act approved by the Minister of the Ministry responsible for internal affairs.

#### Article 6

Obligation of the operator of essential service to report a cyber incident

\1. An operator of essential services shall inform the CSA immediately, but not later than twentyfour (24) hours after becoming aware of a cyber incident:

1.1. which has a significant impact on the security of the system or the continuity of the service;

1.2. a significant impact of which on the security of the system or the continuity of the service is not obvious but can be reasonably presumed.

\2. A cyber incident has a significant impact if at least one of the following conditions is met:

2.1. the impact of the cyber incident is at least high according to the degree of consequences determined in the system risk assessment prepared on the basis of Article 5, paragraph 2 of this Law;

2.2. due to a cyber-incident, the provision of the service cannot be continued after the passing of the maximum permitted time of disruption of the service provided by the relevant service level agreement or requirements for the continuity of the service;

2.3. the continuity of the service of the provider of another service is disrupted due to the cyber incident;

2.4. extraordinary measures set out in the system risk assessment prepared under Article 5 paragraph 2 of this Law or in any other document, if any, which describing the restoration of the continuity of the service or the security of the system need to be applied for resolving the cyber incident;

2.5. the service provider, the provider of another service, or service users suffer or may suffer significant damage due to the cyber incident.

\3. The obligation set forth in paragraph 1 of this Article shall not limit the right of the operators of essential services to notify the CSA of a cyber incident that does not have a significant impact as defined in paragraph 2 of this Article.

\4. Within a reasonable period of time, the operator of essential service is obliged to notify persons possibly affected by the cyber incident with a significant impact, or the public, if the persons affected persons cannot be notified individually.

\5. Based on the information provided in the notification by the operator of essential services, the CSA shall inform all affected operators of essential services if the incident has a significant impact on the continuity of the essential services. The CSA, in accordance with the legislation in force, safeguards the security and commercial interests of the operator of essential services, as well as the confidentiality of the information provided in the notification.

\6. If the operator of essential services fails to comply with the notification obligation provided for in paragraph 4 of this Article within a reasonable period of time, the CSA may notify the person affected or the public itself, also informing the operator of essential services of such notification.

\7. In resolving a cyber incident with significant impact, the operator of essential services is required to submit to the CSA a report which includes information on the causes of the cyber incident, the time spent in its resolution, the measures applied and the impact of the cyber incident.

\8. The Operator of Essential services must maintain user data in accordance with applicable legislation.

\9. When circumstances permit, the CSA shall provide the operator of essential services that has reported the incident with relevant information on the actions taken following its notification, such as information that may support effective incident handling.

\10. For all information systems, to which access is restricted or completely prohibited, the operators of essential services are obliged to make sure that the user is clearly and automatically warned with information, as well as under what conditions the user can use the information system, or that the use of this information system is prohibited, and the legal consequences of the unauthorized access.

\11. Failure to comply with the obligations set out in this Article by the operator of essential services shall be determined as minor offence.

\12. The procedure for notification of the cyber incident and the format of the report will be determined by a sub-legal act approved by the Minister of the Ministry responsible for internal affairs.

#### Article 8

Obligation of the digital service providers to report a cyber incident

\1. A digital service provider shall notify the CSA of a cyber incident that has a significant impact on the digital service provided, immediately after becoming aware of the cyber incident.

\2. In order to determine whether the impact of an incident is significant, the following criteria shall be considered:

2.1. the number of users affected by the cyber incident, in particular users relying on the service for the provision of their services;

2.2. duration of the cyber incident;

2.3. geographical spread in terms of the area affected by the cyber incident;

2.4. the extent of the interruption the service;

2.5. the extent of the impact on economic and social activities.

\3. The notification shall be submitted to the competent authority or computer security incident response team - CERT of the State where:

3.1. the digital service provider has been established;

3.2. the parent company of the group is established in the case of a group;

3.3. the representative appointed by an economic operator from a third country economic operator is located.

\4. The notification shall include information enabling the competent authority or computer security incident response team - CERT to determine any cross-border impact of the cyber incident.

\5. If a cyber incident has a significant impact on the continuity of a digital service in another state, the CSA shall notify the affected state on the basis of information provided by the digital service provider.

#### Article 9

Prevention and resolution of a cyber-incident

\1. Ensuring cyber security, prevention and resolution of a cyber-incident to the extent provided by this law shall be coordinated by the CSA.

\2. For the purpose of ensuring cyber security, the CSA in coordination with the Ministry of Defence, the Regulatory Authority of Electronic and Postal Communications and other security institutions, analyses the risks posed to the security of systems and their impact on the state, society and security of systems.

\3. CSA will establish and maintain an electronic register of cyber security risks for the country.

\4. For the purpose of preventing and resolving a cyber-incident, the CSA in cooperation with the State Cyber Security Training Centre in MD\KSF organizes exercises with operators of essential service and digital service providers.

5.CSA sends alerts to operators of essential service and digital service providers to enable them to take measures to avoid or reduce the impact of a cyber incident.

\6. CSA has the right to exchange information with a foreign state or with the European Union Cyber Security Agency or with another organization regarding the prevention and resolution of a cyber incident for the performance of the functions set out in this Law or an obligation arising from European Union legislation or in cases as provided for in an international agreement, provided that the information exchanged does not harm national security or criminal proceedings.

\7. When exchanging information, the CSA shall take into account the business interests of the operator of essential service or digital service provider and shall abide by the obligation to keep business secrets.

\8. In order to ensure security of information systems and protection of personal data, CSA

#### Article 10

Cyber incident Registry

\1. The Cyber Incident Registry is a database maintained by the CSA, where data describing the occurrence of a cyber incident is entered for the purpose of keeping record of cyber incidents, analysing cyber incidents for resolving them, sending alerts and performing supervisory operations.

\2. Access to the registry is restricted and the registry data is intended for internal use, unless otherwise provided by the legislation into force.

\3. The contents of the Registry shall be determined by a sub-legal act approved by the Minister of the Ministry responsible for internal affairs.

#### Article 11

Supervision and monitoring

\1. State and administrative supervision over the compliance with the requirements set forth in this law and in the legislation established on the basis of this law are exercised by the CSA.

\2. Upon exercising state supervision, the CSA, for the purpose of preventing an imminent serious threat or the elimination of any disruption in the event of a cyber incident, may temporarily restrict or discontinue the use or access to a network or system, with the exception of military systems, provided that all of following conditions are met:

2.1. a cyber incident compromises or harms the security of another system;

2.2. the system administrator is either unable or unable in a timely manner to respond to the serious threat or eliminate the disruption that may result as a consequence from a cyber incident;

2.3. it is not possible to respond to the serious threat or to eliminate the disruptions that may come from a cyber incident using a less infringing response measure;

2.4. a disproportional damage to a person is not caused as a result of the CSA responding to the serious threat or eliminating the disruption originating from the cyber incident.

\3. The procedure for taking the measures referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article, the documentation of the measures taken and the notification sent to the affected entity shall be regulated by a sublegal act approved by the Minister of the Ministry responsible for internal affairs.

Minor offence sanctions

\1. When the operators of essential services fail to take the system security measures provided for in Articles 5, 6, 7 and 8 of this Law, the CSA shall order the taking of corrective measures and shall send warnings.

\2. When operators of essential services and digital service providers fail to implement the measures specified in the paragraph 1 of this Article, CSA imposes fines from fifteen thousand (15,000) euros to thirty thousand (30,000) euros to legal entity violating obligations set out in this Law.

\3. Fines from one thousand (1,000) to one thousand and five hundred (1,500) Euro shall be imposed on the responsible person of the legal person for non-implementation of obligations defined by this law.

#### Article 25

The right to appeal

\1. Against any final decision according to this Law, there is allowed the appeal in compliance with the rules determined in the respective Law on General Administrative Procedure.

\2. Appeals Commission, which is established by the decision of the Minister of Internal Affairs, shall be a superior body to review the appeals according to this Article.

\3. The respective Minister shall, by sub-legal act, determine the composition of the commission and the mandate of the members of the commission. The work and procedure of the Appeals Commission shall be based on the rules determined for collegial bodies in the respective Law on General Administrative Procedure.

#### Article 26

Sub-legal acts The sub-legal acts provided for by this law should be approved within one (1) year after the entry into force of this Law.

#### Article 27

Abrogation Provisions

\1. Upon entry into force of this law, there shall be abrogated:

### LAW NO. 08/L-173 ON CYBER SECURITY

1.1. Articles 5, 6, 7 and 8 of Law No. 03/L-166 on Prevention and Fight of the Cyber Crime;

1.2. Paragraph 21 of Article 10 of the Law No. 04/L-109 on Electronic Communications.

\2. All assets created based on sub-paragraph 1.2 of this Article shall be transferred to CSA.

#### Article 28

Entry into force This Law shall enter into force fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Kosovo.
