# LAW NO. 08/L-166 ON PUBLIC GATHERINGS

### LAW NO. 08/L-166 ON PUBLIC GATHERINGS

### LAW NO. 08/L-166

### ON PUBLIC GATHERINGS

The Assembly of the Republic of Kosovo;

Based on Article 65 (1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Kosovo, Approves:

### LAW ON PUBLIC GATHERINGS

### CHAPTER I

### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

Purpose

\1. The purpose of this Law is to guarantee the exercise of the constitutional right to public gatherings, as well as the right to organize and participate in public events.

\2. This law is also aimed at regulating the time and method of notifying public gatherings and public events, as well as the tasks and responsibilities of organizers and the Kosovo Police for the implementation of this law.

#### Article 2

Scope The provisions of this Law are mandatory for all institutions of the Republic of Kosovo, organizers and participants in public gatherings and public events.

#### Article 3

Definitions

\1. The terms used in this Law shall have the following meaning:

1.1. Public gathering - shall mean the temporary and peaceful presence of two or more individuals, in public places or spaces open to the public, for the purpose of expressing a common opinion, goal or concern on political, social, national, religious or other issues of public interest. Public gatherings shall include gatherings in which the participants stand in a single place or move between different locations;

1.2. Public events - shall mean all commercial and non-commercial events of any kind, including cultural, humanitarian, sports, religious and entertainment activities, of a competitive or non-competitive nature, which are not considered public gatherings, and are held in public places or spaces open to the public;

1.3. Restriction of public gathering – shall mean a written decision restricting the public gathering;

1.4. Prohibition of public gatherings – shall mean a written decision prohibiting a public gathering;

1.5. Urgent gathering – shall mean a public gathering organized as an urgent response to an event and which, according to the organizer, cannot be postponed, therefore, the regular notification procedure cannot be applied;

1.6. Spontaneous gathering – shall mean a public gathering that is held as a prompt, instant response to an immediate event, that does not have an identifiable organizer and which, according to the participants, cannot be postponed or notified;

1.7. Public place – shall be considered squares, boulevards, streets and any other environment that is freely used for the movement of people and vehicles;

1.8. Space open to the public – shall be considered places that are available to people, who under certain conditions must acquire permission to enter, such as stadiums or various halls under public ownership;

1.9. Organizer - shall be any legal or natural person who organizes a public gathering or public event;

1.10. Steward – shall be the person appointed by the organizer of the public gathering or public event, who has a distinctive badge and is responsible for ensuring that the public gathering adheres to the purpose of the organizer;

1.11. Violence – shall mean the use of physical force by the participants against others and vice versa, which is likely to result in injury or death of a person or serious property damage;

1.12. Weapon – shall have the same meaning as defined in the relevant applicable legislation.

### CHAPTER II

### PUBLIC GATHERINGS

#### Article 4

The right to organize and participate in public gatherings

\1. Each person shall have the right to organize and participate in public gatherings, regardless of gender, race, colour, language, religion, nationality, economic or financial, education or social status, political or other beliefs or any other personal circumstances, according to the rules set out in this Law.

\2. This right can only be restricted by law and when it is necessary and proportionate to ensure public order, public health, national security or protection of the rights of others. These restrictions can by no means deny the core of the guaranteed rights for the freedom of public gatherings and the right to organize and participate in public events.

#### Article 5

Places where public gatherings are held

\1. Public gatherings may be held at any place suitable for that purpose.

\2. If notified that more than one public gathering is to take place at the same time and place, or at an approximate time, the police should enable all public gatherings to take place, except from cases when such a thing is impossible for objective reasons.

\3. If a public gathering is announced in order to protest against another public gathering, the police shall enable the public gathering as well as the public counter-gathering to take place.

The police may, by exception, restrict or prohibit the public counter-gathering in line with the provisions of Article 9 and 10 of this Law, only if there is serious risk of violence between the participants of the public gatherings which cannot be mitigated or prevented by the Police.

#### Article 6

Notification of a public gathering

\1. All public gatherings, except from spontaneous gatherings, should be notified to the Kosovo Police in order to ensure the peaceful nature of the public gathering and undertake the required measures for the public gathering to go well.

\2. The Kosovo Police shall publish the standard form for notifying public gatherings and public events on the official webpage.

\3. The public gathering notification form is submitted by the organizer or his/her representative, in hard copy at the nearest police station from the place where the public gathering is to be held, or electronically, no later than ninety-six (96) hours before the public gathering is held.

4.The public gathering notification shall contain:

4.1. purpose, place, day and time of the public gathering and its ending;

4.2. itinerary of the public gathering (if applicable);

4.3. information on the organizer or leader (if applicable);

4.4. number of stewards (if applicable);

4.5. approximate number of participants, and

4.6. list of special items and equipment to be used at the public gathering (if applicable).

\5. For placement of special items, tools or equipment, according to sub-paragraph 4.6. of this Article, consent must be obtained from the relevant municipality where the public gathering is to be held.

#### Article 7

Public Gatherings which do not require notification

\1. Funerals do not need to be notified in advance.

\2. Public Gatherings held indoors in private closed properties, as well as other private properties, may be held unnotified.

\3. The Police shall not enter in private areas where public gatherings take place without the consent of the public gathering organizer or its owner, except in cases necessary to stop an immediate danger to the life and health of participants in the public gathering or other cases defined by the applicable legislation.

#### Article 8

Urgent and spontaneous public gatherings

\1. Notwithstanding Article 6 of this Law, urgent public gatherings may take place without respecting the deadline for notification of ninety-six (96) hours defined by this law.

\2. In case of urgent public gatherings, a written notification must be made which contains the elements defined in paragraph 4. of Article 6 of this Law, as well as the reason for the urgency.

The notification must be made no later than six (6) hours before the time of the public gathering.

\3. Spontaneous public gatherings do not need to be notified, in accordance with Article 6 of this Law.

\4. The Kosovo Police should facilitate a spontaneous public gathering and urgent public gathering which has not been officially notified, even if the public gathering has no known organizers.

#### Article 9

Restriction of public gatherings

\1. The Kosovo Police may, in coordination with the organizer, no later than forty-eight (48) hours before the public gathering, decide to impose restrictions on the time, place and form of the public gathering.

\2. Public gatherings held in the following places may be subject to necessary and proportionate restriction according to paragraph 1. of this Article, if necessary to ensure that they shall not pose additional obstacles:

2.1. near hospitals and places where ambulances may have difficulties entering and the peace of hospital patients will be disturbed;

2.2. near kindergartens or schools during school hours;

2.3. at intersections and highways, so as not to impede the circulation of vehicles;

2.4. in other places considering the time of the public gathering, number of participants and the purpose of the public gathering, where the public gathering may seriously affect a large number of individuals.

\3. The restrictions from paragraphs 1. and 2. of this Article should be legal, proportionate and necessary, to ensure the public order, public health, national security, or protection of the rights of others. These restrictions should not change the purpose of the public gathering or significantly reduce its impact on the target audience of the organizers. Restrictions should also not have an impact in the reduction of the number of participants.

\4. The decision on restriction of the public gathering may be appealed according to the procedure provided by Article 10, paragraphs 3. and 4. of this Law.

#### Article 10

Prohibition of public gatherings

\1. The Kosovo Police may prohibit a public gathering, if provided by law, if it is necessary, proportionate and as a last measure, on the basis of one or more of the following reasons:

1.1. when the purposes of the public gathering incite or call for the use of violence, hatred based on race, nationality, ethnicity, religion, or sexual orientation, gender identity and other personal characteristics in a way that could disrupt public order;

1.2. there are serious grounds for believing that the public gathering will pose a threat to public order, public health, national security or to the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.

\2. For the prohibition according to paragraph 1. of this Article, the Police shall issue a written decision no later than seventy-two (72) hours before the public gathering.

\3. The decision from paragraph 2. of this Article may be appealed within twenty-four (24) hours after receiving the decision, at the Appeal Committee of Public Gatherings at the Ministry responsible for internal affairs.

\4. The Appeal Committee shall make a decision on the appeal within forty-eight (48) hours after the appeal is filed.

\5. A lawsuit may be filed against the decision of the Committee at the competent court.

\6. The Minister of the Ministry responsible for internal affairs, issues a sub-legal act, defining the tasks, responsibilities, composition and mandate of the Committee members.

#### Article 11

Responsibilities of the organizer of the public gathering

\1. The organizer is responsible for organizing and leading the public gathering. The organizer may appoint another person to lead the public gathering.

\2. The organizer is responsible to undertake all necessary measures to ensure that the public gathering continues according to the certain measures and it is also the responsibility of the organizer to cooperate with the Police at all times and by notifying them of any change.

\3. The organizer may ensure the presence of stewards at a public gathering, to assist in its management. Stewards must carry a distinctive colored armband or other identification mark.

\4. The steward must instruct, guide, explain and provide information to participants and assist in identifying potential risks. The role of stewards may also be exercised by private security companies, hired by the organizer.

\5. In case the steward notices any participant or other person in the public gathering disturbing the peace, holding weapons, dangerous objects or prohibited signs, or tries to hinder the public gathering, they must inform immediately the Police.

\6. During the public gathering, the organizer shall create space for passage of the Kosovo Police vehicles, ambulance and firefighters.

\7. The organizer or steward is not responsible for the compensation of damages inflicted on the public or private property, unless they have personally and intentionally incited, caused or participated in their damage.

#### Article 12

Maintaining order in public gatherings

\1. The Police is responsible for maintaining public order and security at public gatherings.

\2. The Police is responsible for ensuring the right to organize, participate and free speech of every person in public gatherings.

\3. The Police may intervene in a public gathering in order to maintain order and take proportionate and necessary measures, only in cases when:

3.1. the way the public gathering proceeds is such that may concretely threaten public order, public health, national security, or the rights of others;

3.2. criminal activities are carried out during the public gathering;

3.3. there is an emergency situation related to public safety or when the space utilized by the public gathering is requested by the emergency services.

\4. The Police may order the removal of a person or group of persons from the public gathering, if they hinder the normal development of the public gathering by using violence.

\5. During the public gathering, the Police may record and take pictures for purposes of identification, if there is a grounded suspicion that a criminal offence was committed, is being committed or it is likely that the criminal offence may be committed.

\6. The records, films and pictures taken in public gatherings shall be stored and destroyed in accordance with the relevant applicable legislation.

\7. The Police may take additional actions for the sake of general security by placing strong barriers and creating security corridors in the places, or in the vicinity of the place where the public gathering is held.

\8. The Police is obliged to facilitate the public gathering that has not been notified but of which they have been made aware, as long as the public gathering is not in contradiction with paragraph 1. of Article 14 of this Law.

#### Article 13

Prohibited actions and items at public gatherings

\1. Participants in public gatherings are prohibited from carrying weapons.

\2. Participants in public gatherings are prohibited from carrying or wearing signs, inscriptions or other distinctive items which incite violence, racial, national, ethnic, religious hatred, based on sexual orientation, gender identity and other personal characteristics.

#### Article 14

Measures to interrupt or disperse a public gathering

\1. The Police may interrupt and disperse a public gathering if:

1.1. there are armed participants in the public gathering, and the Police cannot identify and remove those participants from the public gathering;

1.2. participants publicly incite or disperse hatred, discord or intolerance between national, racial, religious, ethnic and other groups or based on sexual orientation, gender identity and other personal characteristics in a manner that may disrupt public order; and

1.3. it is necessary to protect public order, public health, national security, or the rights of others.

\2. If a public gathering is to be dispersed, the Police orders the organizers, stewards and participants to stop the public gathering and disperse from the place where the public gathering is taking place.

\3. If participants are not dispersed within a reasonable period of time after the order for dispersal by the Police, the dispersal of the public gathering can be done by force. In any case, the Police may use force only if the danger cannot be avoided with less coercive measures.

\4. The use of force by the Police is carried out only when it is necessary and reasonable and based on legal provisions.

### CHAPTER III

### PUBLIC EVENTS

#### Article 15

Public events

\1. Every person, regardless of gender, race, colour, language, religion, nationality, economic and financial, educational and social status, political opinion or other or any type of personal circumstance is entitled to organize and participate in public events, in accordance with the rules provided by this Law.

\2. Legal entities have the right to organize public events in accordance with the rules provided by this Law.

#### Article 16

Places where public events are held Public events can be held in public places or in spaces open to the public, suitable for that purpose. The restrictions of paragraph 2. Article 9 of this Law shall also apply to public events.

#### Article 17

Notification of a public event

\1. The public event must be notified no later than seven (7) days in advance.

\2. The notification for the public event shall be made by completing the public event notification form which is submitted by the organizer or their representative in hard copy to the police station nearest the place where the event is to be held, or electronically.

\3. The notification of the event must contain:

3.1. name, surname, personal number, place of birth and residence and other data of the organizer;

3.2. measures to maintain order, peace and security along with the number of stewards;

3.3. contract with a private security company (if applicable);

3.4. nature of the public event;

3.5. date, time and place of the event;

3.6. approximate number of participants;

3.7. consent for the use of the space by the respective municipality or the institution responsible for managing the space where the public event is to be held.

\4. Based on the notification, the Police shall assess whether there are conditions for holding a public event in terms of security.

\5. Notwithstanding paragraph 1. of this Article, the public event can also be notified in a shorter period of time, if the organization of the public event will not disturb the public order and if the late notification will not unreasonably impede the performance of Police duties.

#### Article 18

Maintaining order at public events

\1. The Police shall be responsible for maintaining public order and security at the public event.

\2. The organizer is responsible for organizing and leading the public event. The organizer may appoint another person to lead the public event. The organizer must ensure the presence of a sufficient number of stewards at the public event to assist the management of the public event.

\3. All provisions of Article 11 of this Law shall also apply to public events.

\4. The provisions of paragraphs 3., 4., 5., 6., 7. and 8. of Article 12 of this Law shall also apply to public events.

#### Article 19

Restriction of public events

\1. No later than five (5) days before the start of the public event, the Police may, in coordination with the organizer, impose restrictions on the time, place and form of the public event.

\2. Restrictions from paragraph 1. of this Article must be legal, proportionate and necessary to ensure public order, public health, national security or protection of the rights of others. These restrictions should not change the purpose of the public event, significantly reduce its impact on the target audience of the organizers or have an impact in the reduction of the number of participants.

#### Article 20

Prohibition of public events

\1. The Police may, by means of a written decision, prohibit the public event no later than five

(5) days before it is held, on the basis of one or more of the reasons set forth in paragraph 1. of Article 10 of this Law.

\2. The decision of the Police to prohibit or restrict a public event can be appealed at the Appeal Committee defined in paragraph 3., Article 10 of this Law within forty-eight (48) hours after receiving the decision.

\3. The Appeal Committee shall make a decision on the appeal within forty-eight (48) hours after the appeal is filed.

\4. A lawsuit may be filed with the competent court against the decision of the Committee.

#### Article 21

Prohibited actions and items at public events The provisions of Article 13 of this Law on prohibited actions and items in public gatherings shall also apply to public events.

#### Article 22

Measures to interrupt or disperse a public event The provisions of Article 14 of this Law on measures to interrupt or disperse public gatherings shall also apply to public events.

#### Article 23

Reimbursement of Police expenses for public events

\1. By the organizer’s request, Police may take additional security measures during public commercial or sport events. In this case, the organizer is responsible for reimbursing the costs incurred by this engagement.

\2. The Minister responsible of internal affairs shall, by a sub-legal act, define the procedures for the implementation of paragraph 1. of this Article.

### CHAPTER IV

### FINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 24

Penal Sanctions

\1. In principle, sanctions imposed after a public gathering or public event are more appropriate measures than imposing restrictions before or during a public gathering or public event.

\2. For violation of the provisions of this Law, the Police shall impose the following fines:

2.1. the organizer of the public gathering, who does not notify the public gathering in accordance with Article 6 and paragraph 2. of Article 8 of this Law, shall be punished by a fine:

2.1.1. in the amount of one hundred (100) Euros when they are a natural person and responsible person of the legal entity;

2.1.2. in the amount of three hundred (300) Euros when they are a legal entity.

2.2. the organizer of a public event, who does not notify the public event in accordance with Article 17 of this Law, shall be punished by a fine:

2.2.1. in the amount of two hundred (200) euros when they are a natural person and responsible person of legal entity;

2.2.2. in the amount of six hundred (600) euros when they are a legal entity.

2.3. the organizer who holds the public gathering and does not respect the decision for the restriction of the public gathering according to Article 9 or for the prohibition of the public gathering shall, in accordance with Article 10 of this Law, be punished by a fine:

2.3.1. in the amount of two hundred (200) Euros when they are a natural person and responsible person of legal entity;

2.3.2. in the amount of four hundred (400) Euros when they are a legal entity.

2.4. the organizer who holds the public event and does not respect the decision for the restriction of the public event according to Article 19 or prohibition of the public event in accordance with Article 20 of this Law, shall be punished by a fine:

2.4.1. in the amount of three hundred (300) Euros when they are a natural person and responsible person of legal entity;

2.4.2. in the amount of nine hundred (900) Euros when they are a legal entity.

2.5. the organizer who does not stop the public gathering in accordance with paragraph

\2. of Article 14 of this Law shall be punished by a fine:

2.5.1. in the amount of two hundred (200) Euros when they are a natural person and responsible person of legal entity;

2.5.2. in the amount of four hundred (400) Euros when they are a legal entity.

2.6. the organizer who does not stop the public event in accordance with Article 22 of this Law, shall be punished by a fine:

2.6.1. in the amount of three hundred (300) Euros when they are a natural person and responsible person of legal entity;

2.6.2. in the amount of nine hundred (900) Euros when they are a legal entity.

\3. Participants who carry weapons in a public gathering or public event, in contradiction with paragraph 1., of Article 13 and Article 21 of this Law, shall be punished:

3.1. by a fine of one thousand (1000) Euros for persons with weapon permits;

\4. The person who acts against paragraph 2. of Article 13 and Article 21 of this Law in public gatherings or public events shall be punished in line with the provisions of the Criminal Code of the Republic of Kosovo.

#### Article 25

Prevalence of the Law In the event of a discrepancy between the provisions of this Law and other laws that contain provisions regarding the organization of public gatherings and events, the provisions of this law shall prevail.

#### Article 26

Sub-legal Acts

\1. Ministry responsible for Internal Affairs, shall issue sub-legal acts for implementation of this Law, within a period of three (3) months, from the day of the entry into force of this Law.

\2. The sub-legal acts to be issued by the Minister responsible for Internal Affairs are as in the following:

2.1. regulation for the procedure on engagement of additional police, according to paragraph 1., Article 23 of this Law;

2.2. regulation on duties, responsibilities, composition and mandate of commission members, according to paragraph 6. of Article 10 of this Law.

#### Article 27

Repeal With the entry into force of this Law, Law no. 03/L-118 on Public Gatherings shall be repealed.

#### Article 28

Entry into force This Law shall enter into force fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Kosovo.
