# LAW NO. 08/L-152 ON ANTI-DOPING

### LAW NO. 08/L-152 ON ANTI-DOPING

### LAW NO. 08/L-152

### ON ANTI-DOPING

The Assembly of the Republic of Kosovo;

Based on Article 65 (1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Kosovo, Approves:

### LAW ON ANTI-DOPING

#### Article 1

Purpose The purpose of this Law is to establish, organize and determine the powers of the Anti-Doping Agency, as well as the basic rules and mechanisms that combat the use of substances and methods of doping in sports to protect the health of athletes, to guarantee justice and equal opportunities in sports competitions and to maintain the integrity of sports, in accordance with the international anti-doping conventions, standards and practices.

#### Article 2

Scope The provisions of this law shall be mandatory for all public institutions of central and local level, the Kosovo Olympic Committee, the Kosovo Paralympic Committee, Kosovo Sports Organizations and other legal and natural persons, including those who exercise public authority under laws of the Republic of Kosovo and have legal obligations for anti-doping.

#### Article 3

Definitions

\1. The terms used in this Law shall have the following meaning:

1.1. Agency (State Anti-Doping Agency): is the only Executive Institution of the State Administration and primarily responsible for enforcing anti-doping rules, managing sample collection, managing test results and managing results at the national level;

1.2. World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA): is an independent international non-profit organization, which leads a collaborative worldwide movement for doping-free sport, composed and funded by the sports movement and governments of the world;

1.3. Doping in sport: is the occurrence of one or more of the anti-doping rule violations set forth in to the Code as transposed in the National Anti-Doping Rules;

1.4. Anti-Doping: is undertaking all measures to prevent, detect and avoid doping;

1.5. CAS - Council of Arbitration for Sport;

1.6. Anti-doping activity: anti-doping education and information, planning the distribution of tests, maintenance of the registered testing pool, managing the athlete biological passports, conducting testing, organizing sample analysis, collection of information and identification of violations, processing of applications for therapeutic use exemption (TUE), management of results, monitoring and enforcing compliance

with any consequences imposed, and all other anti-doping-related activities to be carried out by or on behalf of an Anti-Doping Organization as defined in the World AntiDoping Code and/or WADA International Standards;

1.7. Anti-Doping Organization: WADA or a signatory of the Code that is responsible for adopting the rules for initiating, implementing or enforcing any part of the Doping Control process. This includes, for example, the International Olympic Committee, the International Paralympic Committee, other Major Event Organizations conducting testing at their events, International Federations and National Anti-Doping Organizations;

1.8. World Anti-Doping Code (hereinafter: the Code): The Code is the fundamental and universal document on which the World Anti-Doping Program in sport is based.

The purpose of the Code is to advance anti-doping efforts through the universal harmonization of core anti-doping elements;

1.9. Doping control: all steps and processes from planning the distribution of tests to ultimate disposition of any appeal and the enforcement of consequences, including all steps and processes in between, including but not limited to, testing, identification of violations, whereabouts, TUEs, sample collection and handling, laboratory analysis, results management and identification of violations or procedures related to status violations during ineligibility or provisional suspension;

1.10. International standard: a standard approved by WADA in support of the Code.

Compliance with an international standard as opposed to another alternative standard, practice or procedure will be sufficient to conclude that the procedures addressed by the International Standard were properly performed. International Standards include any Technical Documents issued in accordance with the International Standard;

1.11. Prohibited list: the list identifying prohibited substances and prohibited methods.

Defined in the UNESCO International Convention against Doping in Sport and its version updated annually by WADA, and published on the website of the Anti-Doping Agency, completed and implemented in accordance with the requirements of WADA;

1.12. Registered testing pool: the group of highest-priority Athletes established separately at the international level by International Federations and at the national level by the Anti-Doping Agency, who are subject to focused in-competition and out-ofcompetition testing as part of the test distribution plan of that International Federation or Agency and are therefore required to provide whereabouts information as required by the Code and the International Standard for Testing and Investigations;

1.13. Athlete: is any natural person who participates in sports activities in the Republic of Kosovo, especially any individual who is registered in the relevant national sports federation or competes in the official competition systems of sports federations of national branches or participates in sports events and/or training in the Republic of Kosovo;

1.14. Therapeutic Use Exemption (TUE): a therapeutic use exemption allows an athlete with a medical condition to use a prohibited substance or prohibited method, but only if the conditions set out in the Code and the international standard for therapeutic use exemptions are met;

1.15. Doping Free: the label or stamp "DOPING FREE", placed on the boxes of tested supplements indicates that there is no presence of doping substances from the group of prohibited substances;

1.16. Sports organization: is considered anybody that carries out sports activities;

1.17. Minor Offence Authority: according to this Law, the Anti-Doping Agency is considered a minor offence body for Anti-Doping matters;

1.18. Disqualification of sports results: means that the athlete’s results in a particular competition or event are invalidated, with all resulting consequences including forfeiture of any medals, points and prizes, as further specified in the National Anti-Doping Rules;

1.19. Provisional suspension: means that the athlete or other person is barred temporarily from participating in any Competition or activity prior to the final decision at a hearing conducted under Code and National Anti-Doping Rules;

1.20. Ineligibility: means that the athlete or other person is barred on account of an antidoping rule violation for a specified period of time from participating in any Competition or other activity or funding as provided in Code and National Anti-Doping Rules;

1.21. Financial consequences: indicates a financial sanction imposed for an antidoping rule violation or to recover costs associated with an anti-doping rule violation as further specified in the National Anti-Doping Rules;

1.22. Public disclosure: implies the dissemination or distribution of information to the general public or persons beyond those persons entitled to earlier notification in accordance with Code and the National Anti-Doping Rules.

#### Article 4

The body responsible for anti-doping activities

\1. Anti-Doping Agency, hereinafter The Agency, is the institution responsible to implement anti-doping rules, direct the collection of samples, manage test results and conduct results management at national level.

\2. The National Anti-Doping Rules proposed by the Agency are approved by the Ministry of Sports and are binding for all sports organizations and athletes and any person involved in sports or doping control in the Republic of Kosovo.

\3. In exercising its operational activities and in decision-making, the Agency is independent from sport organizations and the Government.

#### Article 5

Organization of the Anti-Doping Agency

\1. The Agency has the status of an executive agency and operates under the oversight of the relevant Ministry of Sports.

\2. The Agency performs the duties and responsibilities as defined by this Law as well as other legislation of the Republic of Kosovo.

\3. The Agency is headed by the Director, who is responsible for the administration, operation, representation and management of the Agency.

\4. The appointment, dismissal, functions and responsibilities of the director are regulated by the relevant legislation in force.

\5. The structure and organization of the Agency is regulated by a sub-legal act in accordance with the law in force on the organization and functioning of the state administration.

#### Article 6

Role and Duties of the Anti-Doping Agency

\1. The Agency shall implement National Anti-Doping Rules of the Republic of Kosovo that conform with the World Anti-Doping Code and WADA’s International Standards.

\2. The Agency shall cooperate with other relevant national organizations and agencies and other Anti-Doping Organizations.

\3. The Agency shall regulate, determine, organize and perform doping control in-competition and out-of- competition, in accordance with the World Anti-Doping Code and National AntiDoping Rules.

\4. The Agency may collect samples, at any time and in any place, from all athletes who are nationals, residents, license holders or members of sports organizations of the Republic of Kosovo or who are present in the Republic of Kosovo in accordance with this Law, the World Anti-Doping Code and National Anti-Doping Rules.

\5. Products and equipment used to collect samples for doping control are not dedicated to be placed on the market, but only for performance evaluation and are not subject to legal provisions for equipment with import/export permits issued by respective authority.

\6. Performs other anti-doping activities as defined in this Law.

\7. The Agency shall create programs and professional bodies which aim at protecting the health of athletes as well as preserving the integrity of sports.

\8. The Anti-Doping Agency is the authority in the Republic of Kosovo that is responsible for planning, implementing, evaluating and promoting anti-doping education in accordance with the requirements of the International Standard for Education.

\9. Carries out data collection activities related to Anti-Doping and cooperates with the responsible bodies.

\10. Identifies all potential anti-doping rule violations and performs results management activities.

\11. Conducts research and consulting in the field of Anti-Doping.

\12. Cooperates with relevant authorities and organizations at national and international level in the prevention of doping in sport.

\13. Supervises the implementation of this Law and the World Anti-Doping Code in the Republic of Kosovo.

\14. Trains, educates and certifies personnel on sample collection.

\15. The procedure and other issues related to paragraph 14. of this Article are regulated by a sub-legal act issued by the Ministry of Sports.

\16. Doping control can be performed only by authorized and qualified officials, respectively by persons who are authorized by the Agency or recognized by that Agency as equivalent.

\17. The Agency shall determine and supervise the implementation of the procedure of verification and issuance of the mark, label or stamp "DOPING FREE" that must be placed on the tested products that contain food supplements and are safe.

\18. The procedure and other issues related to paragraph 17. of this Article are regulated by a

\19. The Agency in accordance with this law and International Standard for the Protection of Privacy and Personal Information, shall collect, store, protect and perform actions of personal data of athletes and other persons in the database related to doping control, TUE, violation of anti-doping rules and sanctions which is created by the Agency.

\20. In accordance with relevant Legislation and International Standard for the Protection of Privacy and Personal Information, the Agency may put at disposal the data referred to in paragraph 19. of this Article to a foreign authorized Anti-Doping Organization if necessary for them to fulfill obligations under the Code and the International Standards and in accordance with applicable privacy and data protection laws, in particular:

20.1. granting therapeutic use exemptions;

20.2. planning, coordination in the implementation of doping control of athletes, including their whereabouts;

20.3. informing about the results of the doping control performed and determining the responsibility for the violation of the anti-doping rules;

20.4. conducting intelligence and investigations of potential anti-doping rule violations;

20.5. providing anti-doping education.

\21. The Agency is obliged to ensure that personal data do not come into the possession of an unauthorized person, as well as to refuse the disclosure of data if the organization to which the data will be provided does not guarantee proper data protection.

\22. To carry out Anti-Doping activities, the Agency may contract other legal entities or individuals inside or outside the country according to the relevant legislation in force.

\23. The Agency shall provide to the competent State Prosecutor's Office all data and information obtained during the conduct of anti-doping activities on suspicion of the existence of elements of a criminal offence.

\24. The Agency shall cooperate with the competent customs, police and inspectorates in the implementation of measures to limit and prevent the availability of prohibited substances and methods.

\25. Governmental and non-governmental bodies of central and local level, sport organizations, persons and other organizations that have relevant information about abuses in the field of doping should provide the Anti-Doping Agency with the required information.

\26. The Agency shall ensure that the identity of those providing the relevant information remains confidential.

\27. The Agency shall draft the anti-doping strategy, which shall be adopted by the relevant Ministry of Sports.

#### Article 7

Funding of the Anti-Doping Agency

\1. The means for funding the work of the Agency are provided from the budget of the Republic of Kosovo, own source revenues, donations and other alternative funding in accordance with the legislation in force.

\2. The Agency has a special budget code and manages its finances.

\3. All own source revenues generated by the agency are transferred to the state budget and allocated according to the legislation in force.

#### Article 8

Violation of Anti-Doping Rules

\1. Doping in sport is the occurrence of one or more of the anti-doping rule violations set forth in the Code as transposed in the National Anti-Doping Rules.

\2. No anti-doping rule violation proceeding may be commenced against an athlete or other person unless he or she has been notified of an anti-doping rule violation as required by the Code or notification has been reasonably attempted, within ten (10) years from the date the violation is asserted to have occurred.

#### Article 9

Obligations of Sports Organizations and Natural Persons

\1. The obligations defined by this law shall be applicable to all organizations and natural persons engaged in activities related to sports, as defined in Article 2 of this Law.

\2. The obligations defined by this law, shall also be applicable to the doping of animals participating in sports competitions.

\3. Relevant sports organizations in the territory of the Republic of Kosovo as well as natural persons engaged in activities related to sports and/or doping control are obliged to respect and implement anti-doping rules under the Code, the National Anti-Doping Rules of the Agency and the provisions under this law.

\4. Relevant sports organizations as well as natural persons engaged in activities related to sports or doping control are obliged to apply anti-doping rules for animals participating in specific sports, in accordance with the rules of the Agency and the International Federation of the respective Sports.

#### Article 10

Permanent Commissions of the Agency

\1. The following permanent commissions shall be established by the Agency:

1.1. Commission on therapeutic use exemption;

1.2. Disciplinary Commission which complies with operational independence requirements under the Code;

\2. The duties, composition, functioning and compensation of the commissions shall be regulated by a sub-legal act issued by the Government.

#### Article 11

Complaints

\1. Dissatisfied parties, with the decisions of the Commissions according to Article 10, paragraph

\1. of this Law, shall have the right to appeal at the Council of Arbitration for Sports (CAS).

\2. The Council of Arbitration for Sports shall establish a special body for reviewing all complaints related to anti-doping, defined by this Law, the National Anti-Doping Rules and the Code.

\3. For exercising functions according to paragraph 1. and 2. of this Article, the CAS has operational and institutional independence in accordance with the requirements of the World Anti-Doping Code.

#### Article 12

Disciplinary Measures and Penalties

\1. Disciplinary measures imposed for violation of anti-doping rules and violation of measures imposed for violation of anti-doping rules set out in this law, the World Anti-Doping Code and the National Anti-Doping Rules are:

1.1. disqualification of sports results;

1.2. provisional suspension;

1.3. prohibition of participation in competitions;

1.4. financial consequences;

1.5. public disclosure;

1.6. teams in team sports may also be subject to Consequences as provided in the National Anti-Doping Code and Rules.

\2. Disciplinary measures from paragraph 1. of this Article are imposed in accordance with this law, and the rules of the Anti-Doping Agency.

\3. Disciplinary measures imposed for non-compliance of other obligations under this Law and the National Anti-Doping Rules are:

3.1. prohibition of performing activities in the field of sports, as provided for in paragraph

\1. of this Article;

3.2. financial fines: from five hundred (500) to one thousand five hundred (1,500) euros for natural persons, and from ten thousand (10,000) to thirty thousand (30,000) euros for legal persons;

3.3. other measures set out in the sports rules of the respective national and international sports federations.

\4. Disciplinary measures from paragraph 3. of this Article are imposed in accordance with this law, the sports rules of the competent national and international sports federations and the rules of the Anti-Doping Agency.

\5. A person who, for the purposes of doping in sport, gives, prescribes, guides the use of doping substances to the athlete, assists in any other way and enables the athlete to use a doping substance or application of doping methods, shall be treated according to this Law and the criminal legislation in power.

\6. A person who, for the purpose of doping in sport, produces, processes, acquires, sells or offers for sale, or with the intent to sell, buys, stores or distributes or interferes with the sale or purchase of doping substances or otherwise circulates without authorization doping substances, shall be treated according to the applicable criminal law.

\7. In addition to disciplinary measures under paragraphs 1. and 3. of this Article, the athlete, coach or sports expert, who is found to be responsible for the violation of the anti-doping rule, shall

#### Article 13

Transitional provisions With the entry into force of this law, the duties and responsibilities of the Anti-Doping Commission that has been functioning within the Kosovo Olympic Committee shall be transferred to the AntiDoping Agency.

#### Article 14

Bylaws

\1. The bylaws provided for by this law shall be issued within six (6) months after the entry into force of this law.

\2. In the period of no longer than one (1) year after the entry into force of this Law, the Olympic Committee and the Sports Federation shall harmonize their statutes and regulations with this Law and establish Anti-Doping Commissions.

#### Article 15

Entry into force This Law shall enter into force fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Kosovo.
