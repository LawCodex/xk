### CHAPTER I

### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

Purpose of this law This law aims the prevention and treatment of violence against women, domestic violence and gender-based violence, to protect and contribute to the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women, the promotion of gender equality and the empowerment of women, and to build the necessary mechanisms for providing support to victims of all forms of violence regulated by this law.

#### Article 2

Scope of the Law

\1. The provisions of this law are mandatory for all public and private institutions, individuals, civil society and other responsible professionals, to provide assistance, support, protection and preservation of the dignity of victims of all forms of violence, according to the provisions of this Law, other legislation in force in the Republic of Kosovo, as well as the main international conventions and instruments directly applicable in the internal legal system.

\2. This Law shall be applied in the civil and administrative justice system, and where applicable, as a supplementary measure in the criminal justice system in order to supplement the protection from the forms of violence included in this Law.

#### Article 3

Definitions

\1. Terms used in this law shall have the following meanings:

1.1. “Family relations” - relationships between persons who are or have been:

1.1.1. engaged;

1.1.2. married;

1.1.3. or have been in an extramarital union;

1.1.4. in joint economy;

1.1.5. are joint parents of one or more children;

1.1.6. use a common household and are related by blood, affinity, adoption, or are in a custodial relationship, including parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren from the daughter and the son, siblings, paternal and maternal aunts, paternal and maternal uncles, nieces and nephews from brother and sister, cousins;

1.2.”Violence against women” - is understood to be a violation of human rights and a form of discrimination against women, and shall mean all acts of gender-based violence that result in or are likely to result in physical, sexual, psychological or economic harm or suffering of women, including threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether in public or private life;

1.3. “Domestic violence” - shall mean all acts of physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence that occur within the family relationship, or the household unit, regardless of the blood connection or family relationship, or from the former or the current intimate partner, regardless of the fact that the perpetrator of the violence shares or has shared the same residence with the victim, with the aim of violating the dignity and keeping under constant control and position of dependence, the persons against whom violence has been exercised;

1.4.”Gender-based violence against women” - means violence directed against a woman because she is a woman or that affects women disproportionately. Gender-based violence against women includes the causes and the result of the unequal power relationship between women and men as a result of a social problem rather than an individual one;

1.5. “Political Harassment” - political harassment is one or several acts of pressure, persecution, harassment or threats, carried out by a person or a group of people, directly or through third parties, against women candidates, elected, appointed or in the exercise of the political-public function or against their families, with the aim of curtailing, suspending, preventing or limiting the functions inherent in their position, to induce or compel them to perform, against their will, an action or not to act , in the performance of duties or in the exercise of their rights;

1.6. “Political Violence” - political violence means actions, behaviour and/or physical, psychological, sexual aggression committed by a person or a group of people, directly or through third parties, against women candidates, elected, appointed or in the exercise of the political-public function, or against her family, to curtail, suspend, prevent or limit the exercise of her duty or to induce or compel her to perform, against her will, an act or omission, in the performance of duties or in the exercise of their rights;

1.7.”Gender” - means the socially constructed roles, behaviours, activities and attributes that a given society deems appropriate for women and men;

1.8.“Psychological violence” - includes constant control, verbal abuse, isolation, humiliation or emotional abuse through insults, insults, blackmail, intimidation, constant tracking and surveillance, withholding of information, misinformation and threatening behaviour, degrading treatment, threats or similar forms of control or psychological pressure, putting a person under reasonable fear of the use of violence against him or persons in a family relationship, the threat of damage to property or being kept under constant emotional distress;

1.9.”Economic violence” - includes the denial of access or control of basic resources through the restrictions of the means necessary for life, the denial of access to medical services, education or employment, the alienation of common property before/during marriage or cohabitation or after its termination, or other forms of pressure in order to keep a person within a family relationship in an economically dependent position. Economic violence can be expressed as a single form of pressure or combined with other forms of abuse or violence;

1.10. “Continuous control” - means a series of actions aimed at keeping a person under

1.11.”Internet sexual harassment” - is an unwanted verbal, non-verbal or other act of a sexual nature, which has a purpose or effect, violates dignity or creates a threatening, hostile, humiliating or intimidating environment, approach or practice;

1.12. “Battered Person Syndrome” - psychological condition that can develop when a person experiences abuse, usually by an intimate partner. It includes, but is not limited to - spouses, people who are dating, sexual partners, and people who do not have a sexual relationship.

1.13. “Intimate partner” – a person with whom the other person is or has been in a close personal relationship, similar to a couple relationship, which can be characterized by emotional connection, continuous physical or sexual contact and who are not or have not been engaged, married or in an extramarital union;

1.14.”Responsible party” - the person against whom any of the forms of protection defined in this law is sought, or the order for protection, the order for emergency protection or the order for temporary emergency protection has been issued;

1.15. “Victim” - the person against whom any type of violence included in this law has been exercised;

1.16. “Vulnerable victim” - girls and women, children, minors, LGBTQ +, persons with physical and mental disabilities, pregnant woman, elderly and the person whose family relationship or dependence on the responsible party makes the person especially susceptible to repeated victimization, intimidation or retaliation;

1.17. “Non-refoulement”- is a principle on the basis of which the state obliges women and children seeking asylum, persons with refugee status or persons under subsidiary protection not to deport or return them in any way to the borders of the state, in which the direct danger to life and limb exists, from violence against women or domestic violence, or their freedom would be endangered because of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political belief or when they would be subjected to torture, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment;

1.18. “Protected party” - the person for whose benefit the protection order was issued;

1.19. “Survivor” - the person who has overcome domestic violence or violence against women;

1.20.”Authorized representative” - the person who is authorized to protect the interests of the parties in the procedure;

1.21.”Victims’ advocate” - the authorized representative for the protection of the interests of the victim and realization of his/her rights in compliance with this Law and other Laws into force.

1.22. “Protection order” -the order issued by a court decision or by the police, which provides for protective measures for the victim and includes the regular protection order, the emergency protective order and the temporary emergency protective order.

### CHAPTER II

### FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES

#### Article 4

Non-discrimination

\1. Domestic violence, violence against women and gender-based violence is a violation of human rights and freedoms, and constitutes discrimination that restricts them from enjoying rights and freedoms on an equal basis with men.

\2. The implementation of the provisions of this Law by the parties, particularly measures for the protection of victims, shall be ensured without discrimination on the basis of gender, race, colour, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, belonging to a national minority, wealth, birth, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, health status, disability, civil status, immigrant or refugee status or other status.

\3. The measures, activities and services of this Law will be undertaken to implement the principle of equality, to eliminate stereotypes related to gender roles, prevention and protection from all forms of discrimination against women, victims of gender-based violence and victims of domestic violence.

#### Article 5

Priority handling

\1. State institutions, legal persons that perform public authorizations defined by law, functionaries, officials and other authorized persons that perform work on behalf of the state, have the obligation to prioritize all measures and activities to prevent any effect of gender-based violence against women and domestic violence.

\2. The entities from paragraph 1 of this Article are obliged to undertake with priority all the appropriate legislative, administrative, judicial and other measures, to prevent, protect, investigate, punish and ensure fair compensation of the victims, including even the priority division of the joint property for victims of domestic violence.

\3. Officials in institutions that perform activities in the field of social protection, internal affairs, health, child protection and education, as well as courts competent in civil, administrative and criminal procedures for domestic violence, gender-based violence and violence against women are obliged to act urgently, with advanced priority to the interests and needs of the victim.

\4. Treatment with priority shall mean even the increased care in undertaking the necessary measures for the evaluation of dangerousness, by all relevant authorities, with the aim of preventing the recurrence of violence.

#### Article 6

The ban of victimization It is prohibited any action taken or inaction that intentionally or unintentionally punishes or puts in a less favourable position a person who has taken action to protect himself from domestic violence, gender-based violence, respectively, if the person has reported violence, initiated a procedure for protection, or testified during the procedure or otherwise, participated in the procedure for protection, according to the provisions of this law and international conventions, applicable in the internal legal system.

#### Article 7

Adequacy of actions

\1. Public institutions that bear obligations under this law to undertake measures, activities and

Gender integration Public institutions are obliged to include the gender perspective as a criterion which is integrated into all phases of the policy creation process, assessment of conditions, implementation and evaluation of the policies that emerge from this law.

### CHAPTER III

### COMPETENT INSTITUTIONS AND JURISDICTION

#### Article 9

Law enforcement competence

\1. All state administration bodies, courts, prosecution offices, police, centres for social works, victims’ advocates, local self-government units and legal entities that carry out public authorizations defined by law are competent for the implementation of this law.

\2. The entities from paragraph 1 of this Article are obliged to ensure that the work within their competence is carried out by professional persons, trained for the implementation of the work defined by this law.

#### Article 10

General obligations

\1. In order to prevent and eliminate domestic violence and violence against women, institutions of all levels in the Republic of Kosovo are obliged to implement legislative measures and other measures, including, but not limited to:

1.1. adoption of National Strategy for Prevention and Protection against Domestic Violence and action plans in accordance with this Law and the national program for protection from domestic violence, violence against women and gender-based violence;

1.2. placing the rights of victims in the centre during the drafting of all policies, documents and legislation from paragraph 1 of this Article;

1.3. the adoption of measures to promote social and cultural changes in the behaviour of women and men with the aim of eliminating prejudices, customs, traditions and all other practices that are based on the idea of the inferiority of women or on the stereotyped roles of women and men;

#### Article 12

National Strategy on the prevention, protection and treatment of domestic violence, violence against women

\1. The Government approves the National Strategy for Prevention and Protection from Domestic Violence and the action plan.

\2. The National Coordinator leads the drafting process and submits the National Strategy for Protection from Domestic Violence to the Government for approval.

\3. The national strategy defines the institutional tasks and responsibilities for the implementation of policies aimed at prevention, protection, coordination, processing, resocialization and reintegration, all institutions have the obligation to fulfil the tasks and responsibilities arising from the action plan of the strategy.

\4. Drafting of the National Strategy for Protection from Domestic Violence and the action plan is done in accordance with the requirements defined by this Law and Council of Europe Convention on preventing and combating violence against women and domestic violence.

#### Article 16

Responsibilities of the Victim’s Advocate

\1. The duties and responsibilities of the victims advocates under this Law include but are not limited to:

1.1. meeting the victim immediately after the referral of the case of domestic violence to the police;

1.2. informing the victim about his or her rights, the help that is available and protective order procedures;

1.3. compiling the request for a protection order with the consent of the victim and collecting the necessary documentation/evidence to support the request;

1.4. submitting a request for a protection order to the court after obtaining consent from the victim or his or her legal representative;

\2. Any assistance and action related to the procedure of seeking protective orders is based on the consent of the victim.

\3. In addition to the obligations in representing the victim in the procedure of seeking protective orders, the victim’s advocate also informs the victim about his/her right to compensation from the state, guides and assists the victim in submitting the request for compensation in accordance with the Law on the compensation of the victims.

#### Article 19

Responsibilities of the Kosovo Police

\1. The Kosovo Police must respond to each report of acts or threats of domestic violence and gender-based violence or violation of a protection order or emergency protection order, regardless of who makes the report.

\2. The police evaluates the dangerousness of the reported case from the moment of the initial response and takes security measures for the victim, the members who are in a family relationship with him/her and other persons involved in the case.

\3. If there is a reasonable suspicion that an act related to domestic violence and violence against women has been committed, the Kosovo Police detains the alleged perpetrator in accordance with the Law.

\4. The Kosovo Police shall take appropriate measures to prevent violence and to protect victims, including but not limited to:

4.1. providing a special telephone line for reporting domestic violence and violence against women and recording these calls;

4.2. informing the victim, the legal representative or the victim’s advocate about the victim’s rights in accordance with this law, in particular the right to request an order for temporary emergency protection according to Article 28, 52, 53 and 54 of this law;

4.3. informing the victim about legal, psychological and other assistance services offered by government institutions and the authorized network of non-governmental organizations that provide services to victims;

4.4. informing the relevant service providers defined according to this law, and enabling contact between the service provider and the victim at the request of the victim;

#### Article 28

Orders and protective measures

\1. Protection orders are measures issued by the Court, respectively the Police, which aim to prevent violence, protect the victim and other persons exposed to violence, avoid the consequences and avoid the circumstances that affect or may influence the repetition of violence. Protection orders are issued even for persons who are not in a family relationship, as well as for the protection of victims of sexual violence and sexual harassment and other categories protected by this Law.

\2. Regular protection orders may contain one or more protective measures and may be:

2.1. regular protection order is the order issued by the Court in regular procedure. The duration of the regular protective order is up to twelve (12) months, with the possibility of extension, which cannot be longer than twenty-four (24) months. Limitation according to this paragraph does not prohibit the applicant to submit a new request;

2.2. this order is valid until the expiration, termination, or issuance of a new protective order by the court.

2.3. an emergency protection order is an order issued by the Court in an expedited procedure to provide immediate protection to the protected party;

2.4. the emergency temporary protection order is the order issued by the Police outside the working hours of the Court. This order is valid until the first working day of the Court.

\3. Protective measures are applied to all forms of violence provided for by this Law, if they are considered adequate to achieve the goal according to paragraph 1 of this article.

1.2. a warning that the violation of the temporary emergency protection order is a criminal offense;

1.3. the explanation that after the deadline for temporary emergency protection has passed, the protected party can submit a request for an emergency protection order, which, if allowed, would be subject to holding a hearing or submitting a request for a protection order against which an appeal is made.

\2. The order for temporary emergency protection issued by the Kosovo police must be sent immediately to the responsible party in the family in accordance with the law.

\3. The law enforcement authorities shall deliver a copy of the temporary emergency protection order to the following persons:

3.1. the protected party and other persons whose names are listed in the order for emergency protection;

3.2. applicants;

3.3. the local police station in the places where the protected party and other persons mentioned in the order for temporary emergency protection reside on permanent or temporary basis;

3.4. Centre for Social Work in the municipality where the protected party and the other persons mentioned in the temporary emergency protection order reside on permanent or temporary basis;

3.5. the competent court; and

3.6. victims’ advocate or authorized representative.

\4. The temporary emergency protection order shall, immediately upon issuance by the Kosovo Police enter into force and shall begin to be applied on the responsible party after it has been personally delivered to him/her.

#### Article 54

Violation of Protection Orders

\1. Violation of the protection order, emergency protection order or temporary emergency protection order, entirely or partially, constitutes a criminal offense sanctioned according to Criminal Code of the Republic of Kosovo.

\2. Continuous repetition of the violation in whole or in part of the protection order, emergency protection order or temporary emergency protection order shall be considered aggravating circumstances for the perpetrator.

\3. Violation of the protection order, emergency protection order or temporary emergency protection order shall be prosecuted ex officio.

\4. The Kosovo Police shall regularly monitor and implement the protection orders issued by the competent body, depending on the level of dangerousness in compliance with the continuous evaluation of reoccurrence of the violence.

\5. The Kosovo Police executes protection orders issued by the competent authority.

#### Article 55

Prohibition of application of alternative procedures The application of alternative procedures in criminal and administrative procedure for solving cases of domestic violence, violence against women and gender-based violence, but not limited to mediation and reconciliation, is prohibited.

#### Article 56

Orders issued from other countries

\1. The protection order issued by another state shall be registered and enters into force by the courts of Kosovo and shall be implemented by the relevant institutions as if it had been issued in accordance with this law.

\2. The court registers the protection order issued by another state based on the following documents:

2.1. certified copy of the protection order; and/or

2.2. a statement signed by an officer of the court of the other state issuing the order, certifying that the issued order remains in force on the date of certification.

\3. The court reserves the right to keep a certified copy of the protection order for recording purposes

\4. The court is obliged to send a copy of the protection order to the police and all other relevant institutions within twenty-four (24) hours.

#### Article 57

Non-recognition of protection order issued by other states

\1. Courts may decide not to recognize the protection order issued by another country in cases where:

1.1. at the time of submitting the request to the court of Kosovo, the protection order was not binding in the country of its issuance; or

1.2. at the moment of filing the request to the courts of Kosovo, the duration of the protection

#### Article 58

Limiting the issuance of protection orders against persons pushed to commit violence

\1. Except in case of special circumstances which, according to the court, make the issuance of a protection order necessary, protection orders are not issued against children under the age of fourteen (14), persons of third ages in conditions of mental health that limits the possibility of judging reasonable and disabled persons, who are pushed to commit violence.

\2. In case of issuing protection orders against the categories defined in paragraph 1 of this Article, the Court takes into consideration their well-being and decides on:

2.1. Appointment of legal aid to represent them during court proceedings;

2.2. Assignment of custody and related care by service providers as per the needs of the responsible party, belonging to the groups mentioned in paragraph 1 of this Article.

#### Article 59

The relationship of the victim and the protected party with the employers

\1. Victims of domestic violence and protected parties have the right to take fifteen (15) calendar days of paid leave, to address health, legal problems, change of residence, for themselves or for their dependents.

\2. Victims of domestic violence have the right to request up to one (1) year of unpaid leave to address health problems that are a direct consequence of the experienced violence.

\3. Employers have the right to request from their employees, victims of domestic violence, written evidence for the days spent addressing health and legal problems, change of residence, etc.

\4. It is guaranteed for the victims their workplaces shall be kept and they can take unpaid leave up to one (1) year, only if the employee who is a victim of domestic violence has been employed at the workplace for no less than twelve (12) months.

#### Article 60

Protection Measures for Asylum Seekers, Immigrants and Foreign Workers Victims of domestic violence and violence against women

\1. The provisions of this law apply without prejudice and in the same way to asylum seekers, immigrants and foreign workers, and their family members who are in the territory of the Republic of Kosovo.

\2. In case the protected party has obtained a residence permit or temporary residence or a request for granting of asylum status in the territory of the Republic of Kosovo through the application of the responsible party, the competent bodies examine the application for a residence permit of the protected party, separately from the application or the case of the responsible party.

### CHAPTER V

### TRANSITIONAL AND FINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 62

Implementation

\1. The Government of the Republic of Kosovo, six (6) months after the entry into force of this law, issues sub-legal acts arising from this law.

\2. For the cases where the judicial procedure has started before the entry into force of this law, the provisions of Law no. 03/L-182 on Protection from Domestic Violence shall apply.

\3. For all procedural issues that are not regulated by this Law, the provisions of the Law on Contested Procedure are valid and shall apply accordingly.

#### Article 63

Abrogation Upon the entry into force of this Law, the Law no. 03/L-182 on Protection from Domestic violence shall be abrogated.

#### Article 64

Entry into force This Law shall enter into force fifteen (15) days after the publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Kosovo.
